# Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`)

Write in contemporary Brazilian Portuguese, using the current Latin-script
orthography. Prefer vocabulary and constructions natural in educated
Brazilian usage; do not silently substitute European Portuguese forms. Keep a
clear dictionary register and avoid archaic Bible-translation diction unless
the concept itself is explicitly historical.

Use compact noun phrases, adjective phrases, or infinitives for concept
labels. Omit articles where normal in dictionary glosses, but include a
required preposition or reflexive marker. Preserve the difference between
`ser` and `estar`, action and result, agent and patient, causative and
inchoative, and lexical versus merely syntactic uses of `se`. Check verb
regency rather than calquing a source preposition. Contextual glosses may
identify an object or participant when a one-word equivalent would broaden
the branch. Avoid heavy strings of abstract nouns and unnatural compounds.
Lexical-unit glosses must retain the attested category, regency, and
construction-bound scope rather than generalize a contextual reading.

Prefer established Brazilian Portuguese wording over transliteration.
Arabic-derived religious terms should be used only if they are conventional
in Brazilian Portuguese and match the precise sense; community usage may
differ, and familiar terms can carry broader institutional meanings.
Christian theological vocabulary may also add church-specific associations.
Do not reproduce Arabic expressions or source morphology as decoration.
Generate Portuguese independently, never by mechanically mapping Turkish word
order, possessive constructions, light verbs, or suffix-derived abstractions.

Use established Brazilian Portuguese forms for familiar scriptural and
historical names. For names without a conventional form, apply one consistent,
readable transliteration and avoid adding source case endings or honorifics.
Do not replace a proper name with a translated etymology unless the lexical
meaning is explicitly active.

The reviewer should check accentuation, gender and number agreement, verb
regency, reflexivity, and Brazil-specific naturalness. Watch for apparently
close choices such as `fé`, `caridade`, `piedade`, `provação`, and `tentação`
whose common or Christian senses can displace the intended concept. Verify
that a concise label has not lost a participant or evaluative facet, that no
European Portuguese usage slipped in unintentionally, and that the result
does not read like Turkish syntax in Portuguese.
