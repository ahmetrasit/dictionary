# Dari (`prs`)

Write contemporary Standard Dari as used in Afghanistan, in its
Arabic-derived Persian script. Use broadly intelligible formal Dari and avoid
silently replacing it with Iran-specific Persian vocabulary, spelling
preferences, or colloquial Tehran syntax. Where major Afghan regional
variants differ, choose the form most suitable for a national written
dictionary and avoid unmarked dialect forms.

Form concept glosses as compact noun or adjective phrases and idiomatic Dari
verbal expressions. Use ezafe and compound or light verbs naturally, while
preserving transitivity, causativity, agency, state versus transition, and
action versus result. The choice among کردن، شدن، نمودن, and other verbal
elements is semantic and register-sensitive, not mechanically interchangeable.
Contextual glosses may expose a governed participant when a bare word is too
broad. Avoid long learned ezafe chains and Iranian colloquial contractions.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, complement frame, light-verb
collocation, and construction-bound limits rather than promote a local sense.

Arabic-derived vocabulary is a normal part of Dari, and Arabic-derived script
is required here. Still, distinguish an established Dari lexeme from copied
Arabic evidence: do not paste source phrases, preserve Arabic case endings,
or select a rare cognate only because it resembles the source. Religious and
administrative terms may have specifically Afghan, legal, or sectarian
readings. Persian cognates must also be checked for Dari usage. Never
mechanically translate Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or the
source package’s explanatory syntax.

Use conventional Dari forms for established scriptural and historical proper
names. Keep one accepted Afghan spelling for less familiar names; omit Arabic
inflection and unrequested honorifics. Do not substitute a translated
etymology for a proper name unless explicit wordplay requires it.

Review orthography, ezafe, spacing, plural behavior, light-verb collocation,
valency, and distinctly Dari naturalness. Check for accidental Iranianisms,
overly classical wording, and Arabic cognates whose Dari meaning has drifted.
Legitimate Dari words must not be rejected merely for sharing Arabic script,
but verbatim Arabic evidence must not pass as translation. Confirm a concise,
idiomatic Dari gloss with no Turkish syntactic residue.
