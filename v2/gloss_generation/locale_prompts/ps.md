# Pashto (`ps`)

Write in contemporary Standard Pashto using the Pashto form of the
Arabic-derived script. The `ps` tag does not choose a regional standard;
prefer forms broadly recognized across major Afghan and Pakistani written
varieties, and avoid strongly Kandahari- or Yusufzai-specific vocabulary when
a shared literary form exists.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and idiomatic Pashto verbal labels.
Preserve gender, number, case or oblique behavior, postposition government,
transitivity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state, process,
result, agent, and patient. Past transitive constructions and clitic
placement reflect split ergativity; do not infer semantic agency merely from
surface agreement. Choose simple versus compound verbs according to actual
Pashto lexicalization. Contextual glosses may name an argument or postposition
when a short lemma is too polysemous. Avoid long Persian-style constructions
that do not sound naturally Pashto.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, alignment-sensitive argument frame,
compound-verb behavior, and construction-bound limits rather than generalize
a contextual use.

Arabic and Persian loans are legitimate Pashto vocabulary, written in an
Arabic-derived script, but they must have the required Pashto sense and
inflection. Distinguish such words from copied Arabic evidence: never paste a
source phrase, retain Arabic case endings, or select an obscure cognate for
graphic similarity. Religious and legal loans can carry local institutional
meanings. Preserve Pashto-specific letters and spelling. Never mechanically
copy Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or explanatory phrasing.

Use conventional Pashto forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. For less familiar names, use one consistent Pashto spelling without
source inflection or unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a name with its
etymology unless explicit contextual wordplay activates it.

Review Pashto letter choice, dialect neutrality, gender, oblique forms,
postpositions, valency, causativity, and compound-verb naturalness. Check
Arabic/Persian loans for Pashto semantic drift and do not flag legitimate
Pashto-script vocabulary merely because it resembles Arabic. Reject Dari,
Urdu, or Turkish calques. Confirm a concise, idiomatic Pashto gloss that
preserves semantic roles despite the language’s alignment patterns.
