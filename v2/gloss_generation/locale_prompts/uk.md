# Ukrainian (`uk`)

Write in contemporary Standard Ukrainian using the Ukrainian Cyrillic
alphabet. Keep Ukrainian distinct from Russian in vocabulary, spelling, and
collocation, and avoid Church Slavonic or archaic scriptural register unless
the concept specifically requires it.

Use compact nouns or noun phrases, adjective phrases, or infinitives for
concept glosses. Preserve verbal aspect, reflexive `-ся`, case government,
animacy, and gender where they affect sense. Distinguish action from result,
state from transition, and agent from patient; do not use a perfective verb
for a branch that denotes an ongoing disposition. Contextual glosses may add
the governed object or preposition when a one-word choice would conceal
valency. Prefer a natural Ukrainian analytic phrase over an opaque coined
abstract noun, but do not expand into a definition unnecessarily. Lexical-unit
glosses must preserve aspect, category, argument frame, and any
construction-bound restriction rather than generalize a local reading.

Prefer established Ukrainian terms. Arabic or Islamic loans should be used
only in their attested Ukrainian form and only if their Ukrainian meaning
matches the concept; media and academic usage can be narrower or politicized.
Watch equally for Christian and Church Slavonic vocabulary that adds
confessional implications. Arabic wording must not be copied or merely
transliterated when an idiomatic gloss is available. Never mechanically carry
over Turkish word order, possessive chains, light verbs, or suffix-derived
abstractions. Do not route the translation through Russian.

Use conventional Ukrainian forms of established scriptural and historical
names. For other names, follow a consistent Ukrainian transliteration and
phonological adaptation, not Russian spelling. Omit Arabic case endings and
unrequested honorifics. A proper name remains a name unless its lexical
meaning is explicitly active in context.

The reviewer should check the Ukrainian alphabet (`і`, `ї`, `є`, `ґ` where
appropriate), Russianisms, aspect, case government, reflexivity, animacy, and
semantic role. Scrutinize religious terms whose Church usage can broaden or
narrow the branch. Confirm that the phrase is natural Ukrainian, not a
Russian calque, Turkish syntax, or raw Arabic transliteration, and that its
brevity has not erased a source facet.
