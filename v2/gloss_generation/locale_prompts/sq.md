# Albanian (`sq`)

Write contemporary Standard Albanian, based on the standard Tosk norm, in
Latin script. Use `ë` and `ç` correctly and avoid unmarked regional Geg forms
unless no standard equivalent exists. Keep a modern lexicographic register,
not archaic religious or nationalist prose.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases. Standard Albanian lacks the productive
Geg `me` infinitive; for verbal concepts, use a natural `të` plus subjunctive
label or another accepted standard dictionary formulation rather than
inventing an infinitive. Preserve grammatical gender, definite versus
indefinite form, case and preposition government, voice, reflexivity,
reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state, process,
result, agent, and patient. Contextual glosses may expose an object or
relation when a short lemma is too broad. Do not overuse nominalizations or
stack genitives in source order.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, case or prepositional frame, voice,
and construction-bound limits rather than generalize a local reading.

Arabic and Persian religious vocabulary often entered Albanian through
Ottoman Turkish, so formal resemblance can conceal several layers of semantic
drift. Use such a word only if its current Standard Albanian sense and
register fit; community, historical, and dialect uses may differ. Equally,
Christian terminology can add Catholic or Orthodox associations. Do not copy
Arabic evidence or revive a Turkish loan solely because it resembles the
source. Never mechanically transfer Turkish syntax, compounds, light verbs,
or suffixal abstractions into Albanian.

Use conventional Albanian forms for familiar scriptural and historical
proper names. Render less established Arabic names consistently according to
Albanian spelling, without source case endings or unrequested honorifics. Do
not replace a proper name with its translated etymology unless wordplay is
explicit.

Review `ë`/`ç`, definiteness, gender, number, case, prepositions, verbal
formulation, reflexivity, and semantic roles. Check Ottoman-era loans for
modern Albanian drift and for Turkish-calque behavior; also reject accidental
Geg-only or neighboring-language forms. Confirm that the phrase is concise,
idiomatic Standard Albanian and not Turkish thought expressed with Albanian
endings.
