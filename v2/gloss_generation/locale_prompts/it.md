# Italian (`it`)

Use contemporary Standard Italian in Latin script, with a neutral
lexicographic register. Avoid strongly regional vocabulary and archaic
language inherited from older Bible or literary translations unless the
represented concept itself calls for it.

Concept glosses should usually be compact noun phrases, adjective phrases, or
infinitives. Articles may be omitted in dictionary labels when natural, but
retain required prepositions and pronominal particles. Preserve grammatical
gender, transitivity, reflexive or reciprocal `si`, and distinctions among
state, process, action, result, agent, and patient. Do not flatten an
inchoative into a passive or use a generic `fare` construction for a precise
causative. Contextual glosses may name an argument or relation when the bare
Italian lemma has several competing frames. Avoid ornate nominalizations and
long source-shaped `di` chains. Lexical-unit glosses must keep the attested
category, argument frame, and construction restriction rather than promote a
context-only use.

Prefer established Italian vocabulary. Arabic or Islamic loans are
appropriate only when current Italian usage and the represented facets
coincide; terms used mainly in journalism, law, or confessional discourse may
carry unwanted modern associations. A transliterated Arabic word is not
automatically a precise gloss, and Arabic evidence must not be copied into
Italian text. Christian theological near-equivalents also require scrutiny.
Never mechanically reproduce Turkish word order, compound structure,
light-verb constructions, or suffixal abstractions.

Use conventional Italian forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. For less familiar names, use one readable Italian-oriented
transliteration without source inflections or added honorifics. Preserve the
status of a proper name; do not substitute its etymological meaning unless the
context explicitly exploits it.

Review articles, gender and number agreement, preposition choice, pronominal
verbs, and semantic role assignment. Check whether vocabulary such as `fede`,
`carità`, `pietà`, `prova`, or `tentazione` imports Catholic or everyday
meanings not present in the source branch. Reject elegant but broader literary
synonyms, awkward literal calques, and wording that is grammatical yet
non-lexicographic. Confirm compact, idiomatic Italian with no Turkish
syntactic skeleton.
