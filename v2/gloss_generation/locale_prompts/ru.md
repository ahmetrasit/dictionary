# Russian (`ru`)

Use contemporary Standard Russian in Cyrillic script and a neutral
lexicographic register. Avoid Church Slavonic, prerevolutionary, or elevated
scriptural diction unless historically required by the represented concept.

Concept glosses should normally be concise nouns or noun phrases, adjective
phrases, or infinitives. Preserve verbal aspect, reflexive `-ся`, case
government, animacy, and distinctions among action, process, state, result,
agent, and patient. A perfective infinitive can imply a bounded result that an
imperfective source branch lacks; choose deliberately. Contextual glosses may
state the governed participant or preposition when a bare lemma has several
frames. Avoid overusing abstract nouns in `-ние` and source-shaped genitive
chains merely to achieve brevity. Lexical-unit glosses must retain aspect,
category, argument frame, and construction-bound scope rather than promote a
contextual use.

Prefer established Russian vocabulary. Arabic-Islamic loanwords may be
standard in scholarship, community use, or news reporting but still have a
narrower, broader, or politically colored Russian sense. Use them only after
checking conceptual fit; transliteration alone is not translation. Orthodox
and Church Slavonic near-equivalents can likewise introduce confessional
meanings. Arabic evidence is not target text. Independently formulate Russian
and never mechanically copy Turkish word order, possessive compounds,
light-verb expressions, or nominalizing suffixes.

Use conventional Russian forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. For less familiar Arabic names, follow one consistent Russian
transcription, without source case endings or added honorifics. Do not
translate a name’s etymology or silently use a name form associated with a
different figure.

Review aspect, case, reflexivity, animacy, gender, valency, and headedness.
Check whether a term carries Soviet administrative, Orthodox theological, or
modern political associations absent from the concept. Watch high-risk broad
words such as `вера`, `милостыня`, `благочестие`, and `искушение`. Reject
fluent wording that shifts an event into a result or a quality into an agent.
The final phrase must be concise, idiomatic Russian and free of Turkish
syntactic residue or copied Arabic material.
