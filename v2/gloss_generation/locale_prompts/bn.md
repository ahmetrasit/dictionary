# Bangla (`bn`)

Write contemporary Standard Bangla in the Bengali script. Because `bn` has no
region subtag, prefer formal vocabulary and spellings broadly intelligible in
Bangladesh and West Bengal; avoid strongly regional colloquialisms and
unnecessarily polarized Sanskritized or Perso-Arabic registers.

Use concise noun or adjective phrases and natural verbal labels. Preserve the
difference among action, state, process, result, agent, and patient. Bangla
compound verbs, causatives, conjunctive constructions, classifiers, and
honorific levels can alter event structure or participant status; include
only what the concept requires. A dictionary gloss normally does not need a
finite honorific verb. Contextual glosses may specify a postposition, object,
or participant when a bare root has several frames. Avoid literal strings of
genitive `-এর/-র` and dense learned compounds that are grammatical but not
idiomatic.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, postpositional or compound-verb
frame, and construction-bound scope rather than generalize a contextual use.

Arabic and Persian loans are established in Bangla, especially in Muslim
usage, but their Bangla pronunciation, spelling, and meaning—not source
appearance—govern their use. Do not insert Arabic script or raw
transliteration, and do not select a rare loan solely because it echoes the
Arabic evidence. Sanskritic near-synonyms may likewise import Hindu
philosophical or highly literary associations. Seek an ordinary exact Bangla
term where possible. Never mechanically copy Turkish word order, possessive
patterns, light verbs, or suffixal abstractions.

Use conventional Bangla forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names, respecting established Bangla phonological adaptation. For unfamiliar
names, transliterate consistently into Bengali script without source case
endings or added honorifics. Do not translate a name’s etymology unless the
context explicitly activates it.

Review spelling, vowel signs, conjuncts, classifiers, causativity, postposition
government, compound-verb meaning, and Bangladesh/West Bengal neutrality.
Check both Arabic-derived and Sanskrit-derived terminology for community- or
tradition-specific drift. Reject Hindi-shaped, English-shaped, or
Turkish-shaped calques. Confirm that the result is a compact, natural Bangla
gloss that retains semantic roles and does not confuse a legitimate Bangla
loan with copied Arabic.
