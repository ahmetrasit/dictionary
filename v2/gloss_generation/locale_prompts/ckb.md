# Central Kurdish / Sorani (`ckb`)

Write contemporary Central Kurdish (Sorani) in the standard Sorani
Arabic-derived alphabet. Use broadly intelligible formal usage associated
with Iraqi Kurdistan and adjacent Sorani-writing communities. Orthographic
practice is not perfectly unified, so choose one consistent common standard
and avoid strongly local forms.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and idiomatic verbal labels. Preserve
Sorani definiteness and indefiniteness suffixes where semantically required,
plural marking, ezafe/linking constructions, clitic placement, transitivity,
and distinctions among action, state, process, result, agent, and patient.
Past transitive constructions can distribute person marking differently from
present constructions; do not infer semantic roles from surface position
alone. Contextual glosses may state a participant or prepositional relation
when a bare word has several frames. Avoid long Persian-style chains that
obscure the Kurdish head.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, clitic and argument frame, and
construction-bound scope rather than promote a contextual use.

Arabic- and Persian-derived words are legitimate Sorani vocabulary in an
Arabic-derived script, but source resemblance is not proof of semantic fit.
Distinguish natural Kurdish wording from copied Arabic evidence: do not paste
Arabic phrases, retain source inflection, or select a rare cognate merely for
graphic similarity. Religious and political terms may have local
institutional meanings. Avoid substituting Persian or Arabic syntax. Never
mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or suffix
patterns; contact and cognacy can make such calques deceptively intelligible.

Use conventional Sorani forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. Spell less familiar names consistently in Sorani without Arabic
case endings or unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a name with its
etymology unless explicit contextual wordplay activates it.

Review Sorani letters and spacing, suffix attachment, ezafe, clitics,
alignment-sensitive role assignment, valency, and dialect neutrality. Check
Arabic and Persian loans for Kurdish semantic drift and distinguish legitimate
target-script vocabulary from verbatim source leakage. Reject Kurmanji,
Persian, Arabic, or Turkish calques. Confirm a compact, idiomatic Sorani gloss
that retains the intended semantic center.
