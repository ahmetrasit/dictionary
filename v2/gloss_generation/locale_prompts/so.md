# Somali (`so`)

Write contemporary Standard Somali using the official Latin alphabet and
standard Somali spelling. Prefer forms broadly intelligible across Somali
regions and avoid strongly local dialect vocabulary when a common written
equivalent exists. Use a plain lexicographic register rather than poetic or
clan-specific phrasing.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural verbal labels. Preserve
noun gender, number behavior, definiteness where semantically relevant,
transitivity, causativity, reflexivity, reciprocity, and distinctions among
action, state, process, result, agent, and patient. Somali noun gender and
agreement can interact with number, so do not assume a plural has the same
agreement profile as its singular. Focus particles and full-clause subject
markers are normally unnecessary in a dictionary label; do not add them
unless the contextual gloss truly forms a clause. Contextual glosses may
state a governed participant or relation when a bare verb or noun is too
polysemous.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, derivational extension, valency,
and construction-bound scope rather than elevate a contextual use.

Arabic loans are well established in Somali, especially in religious and
scholarly vocabulary, but their Somali spelling, pronunciation, and meaning
govern their use. Do not insert Arabic script or raw transliteration, and do
not choose an uncommon cognate merely because it resembles the source.
Religious loans may have specifically Somali ritual or institutional senses,
while Italian and English loans may add modern administrative meanings.
Never mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or
nominalizing patterns.

Use conventional Somali forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. For other names, apply a consistent Somali Latin rendering,
without Arabic case endings, gratuitous punctuation, or unrequested
honorifics. Do not translate a name’s etymology unless explicit wordplay
activates it.

Review Somali orthography, doubled vowels and consonants, gender, number,
agreement implications, verb valency, and derivational extensions. Check
Arabic-derived terms for Somali semantic drift and distinguish established
loans from source leakage. Reject Arabic-script fragments, English-shaped or
Turkish-shaped calques, and dialectally narrow choices. Confirm a concise,
idiomatic Standard Somali gloss with the correct semantic head and roles.
