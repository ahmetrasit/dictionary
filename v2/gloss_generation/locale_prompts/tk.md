# Turkmen (`tk`)

Write contemporary Standard Turkmen in the official Latin alphabet. Preserve
Turkmen letters and orthography, including `ä`, `ç`, `ň`, `ö`, `ş`, `ü`, `ý`,
and `ž`. Do not use Soviet Cyrillic, Turkey Turkish spelling, or ad hoc ASCII
substitutions.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and `-mak/-mek` infinitives for verbal
concepts when natural. Preserve vowel harmony, case government, possessive
marking, voice, reflexivity, reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among
action, state, process, result, agent, and patient. Turkmen participles,
verbal nouns, and compound verbs can compress argument structure; verify
their aspectual and modal implications rather than treating them as neutral
shortcuts. Contextual glosses may specify an object or complement where a
bare root has an unsafe range.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, suffixation, valency, and
construction-bound scope rather than promote a context-only reading.

Turkish similarity must not drive the wording. Never mechanically map Turkish
word order, compounds, suffixes, light verbs, or idioms into Turkmen.
Cognates can be false friends or differ substantially in frequency, register,
valency, and connotation. Check Azerbaijani and Uzbek lookalikes as well.
Arabic and Persian religious loans may carry specifically Turkmen meanings,
while Russian-mediated vocabulary can introduce administrative or Soviet
associations. Use attested Turkmen senses and do not copy or romanize Arabic
evidence in place of a gloss.

Use conventional Turkmen forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. Render less familiar names consistently in Turkmen Latin
orthography without Arabic case endings, Russian intermediary forms, or
unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a name with its etymology unless
contextual wordplay is explicit.

Review the alphabet, vowel harmony, case, possessives, voice, causativity,
compound-verb collocation, and semantic category. Examine every
Turkish-looking choice for false-friend and Turkish-calque risk, and check
Russian, Arabic, and Persian loans for semantic drift. Confirm that the final
label is concise, idiomatic Standard Turkmen and preserves the intended roles
independently of Turkish.
