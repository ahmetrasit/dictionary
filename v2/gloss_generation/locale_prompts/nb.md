# Norwegian Bokmål (`nb`)

Write contemporary Norwegian Bokmål in Latin script. The locale is `nb`, not
generic `no` and not Nynorsk: use accepted Bokmål forms consistently. Where
Bokmål permits parallel forms, prefer widely used neutral forms and do not mix
radical and conservative morphology haphazardly. Avoid archaic Bible register.

Concept glosses should normally be compact nouns or noun phrases, adjective
phrases, or infinitives. Established compounds are useful, but verify
headedness and linking elements rather than constructing a source-shaped
compound. Preserve particle verbs, reflexive forms, definiteness, valency, and
the distinction between action, state, result, agent, and patient. Articles
can usually be omitted in dictionary labels; required prepositions and
participants cannot. A contextual gloss may disambiguate the object or
relation when a bare Bokmål lemma is too broad. Avoid excessive abstract nouns
in `-het` or `-else`. Lexical-unit glosses must preserve particle structure,
category, valency, and construction-bound scope rather than promote a local
use to the concept.

Prefer established Bokmål vocabulary. Arabic-Islamic loans familiar from
religious or media discourse may have specialized Norwegian meanings; use
them only when those meanings fit exactly. Christian terminology can also add
Lutheran or institutional implications. Transliteration is not a substitute
for a natural gloss, and Arabic evidence must not be copied into the target.
Never mechanically transfer Turkish word order, possessive compounds,
light-verb constructions, or suffixal abstractions.

Use conventional Bokmål forms for well-known scriptural and historical proper
names. Apply one consistent Norwegian-readable transliteration to less
familiar Arabic names, without case endings, explanatory translations, or
unrequested honorifics. Do not treat an etymological meaning as the name’s
gloss unless wordplay is explicit.

Review Bokmål morphology, chosen optional forms, compound structure,
definiteness, reflexivity, preposition government, and semantic roles. Check
high-risk terms such as `tro`, `barmhjertighet`, `fromhet`, and `fristelse`
for inherited Christian or everyday senses. Reject accidental Nynorsk,
Danish-looking archaism, and fluent but overbroad wording. Confirm concise,
natural Bokmål with no copied Arabic evidence or Turkish syntactic skeleton.
