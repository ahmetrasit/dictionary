# Dutch (`nl`)

Write in contemporary Standard Dutch in Latin script. Because `nl` has no
region, prefer forms shared by the Netherlands and Dutch-speaking Belgium;
avoid markedly Netherlandic or Flemish colloquialisms when a neutral standard
choice exists. Use modern dictionary language, not archaic Statenvertaling
register.

Form concept glosses as concise nouns or noun phrases, adjective phrases, or
infinitives. Dutch compounds are welcome when established and transparent,
but do not invent a dense compound that obscures semantic relations. Preserve
separable prefixes, reflexive `zich`, required prepositions, and distinctions
between action, state, result, agent, and patient. Check whether a verb is
transitive, intransitive, reciprocal, or causative in actual Dutch. Articles
are generally omitted in labels unless needed to identify countability or
reference. A contextual gloss may expose an argument that a bare headword
would leave dangerously open. Lexical-unit glosses must preserve separability,
category, valency, and any construction-bound limit rather than generalize a
local use.

Prefer established Dutch equivalents to Arabic transliteration. Islamic loans
may be conventional in community discourse yet narrower, broader, or more
institutional than the source concept. Test them against ordinary Dutch usage
and define rather than merely romanize when necessary. Conversely, inherited
Christian vocabulary can introduce church-specific readings. Do not copy
Arabic wording or form pseudo-Dutch source calques. Never mechanically map
Turkish compound order, possessive constructions, light verbs, or
nominalizing suffixes onto Dutch.

Use conventional Dutch forms for familiar proper names. Apply one consistent,
readable transliteration to less familiar Arabic names, without case endings
or honorific additions. Do not translate the lexical meaning of a name unless
wordplay is an explicit part of the sense.

Review `de`/`het` implications where relevant, plural and diminutive
associations, separable prefixes, reflexivity, preposition government, and
compound headedness. Watch for false equivalence among religious terms and
for words such as `geloof`, `liefdadigheid`, `vroomheid`, and `beproeving`
whose customary meanings may add or omit facets. Confirm that the gloss is
natural across standard varieties, lexically compact, semantically bounded,
and not Turkish phrasing recoded in Dutch.
