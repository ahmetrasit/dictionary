# Hausa (`ha`)

Write contemporary Standard Hausa in Latin `Boko` orthography. Use
Hausa-specific letters such as `ɓ`, `ɗ`, `ƙ`, and `ƴ` correctly. The standard
orthography normally leaves tone and vowel length unmarked, so do not add
specialist tone marks to ordinary glosses. This locale is Latin-script Hausa,
not Ajami.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural verbal labels. Preserve
grammatical gender, number, genitive linking, transitivity, causativity, and
distinctions among action, state, process, result, agent, and patient. Hausa
plural formation is lexically varied; do not invent a plural when a singular
or mass label is intended. Verb grades, object-sensitive forms, and
derivational extensions can alter direction, total affectedness, or valency,
so select forms by actual Hausa usage. Full aspect-pronoun complexes are
usually unnecessary in a dictionary label. Contextual glosses may name an
object or relation when a short item is too broad.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, verb grade or genitive frame, and
construction-bound limits rather than generalize a contextual reading.

Arabic loans are deeply established through Islamic scholarship and Ajami
traditions, but a loan’s Hausa pronunciation, Boko spelling, morphology, and
meaning determine its fitness. Do not output Arabic script, raw
transliteration, or a rare learned cognate simply because it matches the
Arabic evidence. Familiar religious terms may have narrower institutional or
ritual senses in Hausa. English loans can also add modern administrative
scope. Never mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or
suffix-derived abstractions.

Use conventional Hausa forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. Render less familiar names consistently in Boko without Arabic
case endings, Ajami fragments, or unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a
name with its etymological meaning unless explicit wordplay requires it.

Review Boko spelling and hooked letters, gender, plural implications,
genitive linkers, verb grade and valency, and regional neutrality. Check every
Arabic-derived term for actual Hausa semantic range and distinguish
established borrowing from source leakage. Reject English-shaped and
Turkish-shaped calques. Confirm a concise, idiomatic Standard Hausa gloss
whose unmarked tone does not mask a known lexical collision.
