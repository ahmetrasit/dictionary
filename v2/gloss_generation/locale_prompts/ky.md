# Kyrgyz (`ky`)

Write contemporary Standard Kyrgyz in the official Kyrgyz Cyrillic alphabet,
including `ң`, `ө`, and `ү`. Use broadly accepted literary Kyrgyz rather than
strong regional or colloquial forms, and do not substitute Kazakh or Russian
orthography.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural infinitival verbal labels.
Preserve vowel harmony, case government, possessive marking, voice,
reflexivity, reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state,
process, result, agent, and patient. Participles, converbs, and verbal nouns
encode aspectual and participant relations and must not be exchanged merely
for brevity. Contextual glosses may name a governed object or relation when a
bare root is too broad. Select auxiliary and compound verbs according to
Kyrgyz collocation, not by literal correspondence.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, suffixation, argument frame, and
construction-bound limits rather than generalize a contextual use.

Treat Turkish and other Turkic languages as sources of false confidence.
Never mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds, suffix choices, or
idioms. Close cognates may differ in core sense, register, valency, or
frequency, and a Kazakh-looking alternative is not automatically Kyrgyz.
Arabic and Persian religious vocabulary often arrived through regional
contact, while Russian terms can add Soviet or administrative meanings.
Check the Kyrgyz sense independently and do not transliterate Arabic evidence
as a target gloss.

Use conventional Kyrgyz forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. For other names, use consistent Kyrgyz Cyrillic adaptation rather than
unexamined Russian transcription; omit Arabic case endings and unrequested
honorifics. Do not replace a proper name with its etymological meaning unless
explicit wordplay activates it.

Review Kyrgyz letters, vowel harmony, case, possessives, voice, valency,
aspectual implications, and compound headedness. Test Turkish-like cognates
for false-friend behavior and flag Turkish-calqued phrases even when
understandable. Check Russian, Arabic, and Persian loans for target-language
semantic drift. Confirm a compact, idiomatic Kyrgyz dictionary gloss with
correct participant roles and no Turkish syntactic skeleton.
