# Turkish (`tr`)

Write in contemporary Standard Turkey Turkish using the official Latin
alphabet. Use a compact modern lexicographic register; avoid Ottomanizing the
wording, archaism, and sect-specific terminology unless the represented
concept requires them.

Even though Turkish material guides the semantic analysis, do not copy a
source definition into the gloss. Reconstruct a concise Turkish label for the
bounded concept. Use nouns or noun phrases, adjectives, and `-mak/-mek`
infinitives as appropriate. Preserve case government, voice, reflexivity,
reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state, process,
result, agent, and patient. Turkish can compress relations into participles,
possessive compounds, and deverbal nouns, but compression must not hide a
participant or create scope ambiguity. Contextual glosses may state an object
or complement when a bare lemma has multiple valency frames. Apply vowel
harmony and consonant alternation correctly in inflected expressions.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain the attested category, voice, valency, and
construction-bound scope rather than generalize a contextual reading.

Arabic- and Persian-derived words are often fully legitimate Turkish, but
their modern Turkish meanings may have narrowed, broadened, secularized, or
acquired institutional religious senses. Do not assume that an Arabic-looking
Turkish cognate preserves the Arabic source boundary. Prefer an established
plain Turkish equivalent when it is more exact, while avoiding ideological
purism or invented substitutions. Arabic evidence is not target text; do not
insert source forms, Arabic script, or Ottoman spellings merely because the
locale is Turkish. Also avoid mechanically retaining the source package’s
explanatory syntax, facet order, or light-verb phrasing.

Use conventional Turkish forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. For less familiar names, use a consistent modern Turkish
transcription, without Arabic case endings or unrequested honorifics. Do not
replace a name with its etymological meaning unless the context explicitly
activates that meaning.

Review suffix scope, compound headedness, possessive marking, case, voice,
valency, and the lexical status of light-verb constructions. Scrutinize
high-risk Arabic loans for semantic drift and modern theological or
administrative meanings. Confirm that the label is a genuine short Turkish
gloss rather than a clipped copy of the Turkish source explanation, and that
it preserves every essential role and contrast.
