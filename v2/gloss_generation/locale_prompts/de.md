# German (`de`)

Use contemporary Standard German (Standarddeutsch) in Latin script. The bare
`de` tag is region-neutral, so avoid vocabulary restricted to Germany,
Austria, or Switzerland when a generally understood standard form is
available. Prefer natural lexicographic German over elevated Luther-Bible
register or needlessly bureaucratic compounds.

Form concept glosses as compact nouns or noun phrases, predicative adjective
phrases, or infinitives. Capitalize German nouns. Include an article only when
it prevents a gender or countability ambiguity; otherwise dictionary-style
labels may omit it. Keep separable-prefix and reflexive verbs intact, including
`sich` when it changes valency or sense. Distinguish state from event, process
from result, and agent noun from action noun. German compounds are useful when
lexicalized and transparent, but unpack a long nonce compound if it hides a
facet or produces the wrong head. Contextual glosses should make governed
prepositions and participants explicit where needed. Lexical-unit glosses
must retain the unit’s category, valency, and construction-bound character
rather than promote a local reading to the concept label.

Prefer established German vocabulary. Treat Arabic religious or legal
loanwords cautiously: a loan familiar in Islamic discourse may be unknown in
general German or may carry a narrower confessional meaning. Transliteration
is not a substitute for a gloss. Avoid literal Arabic genitives and
capitalized theological calques that are not idiomatic German. Do not
mechanically copy Turkish compound order, nominalizations in `-lik`, light-verb
constructions, or Turkish religious formulas. Cognate-looking international
terms must still be checked in German usage.

Use conventional German forms for established proper names, especially
biblical and widely known historical figures. Use one consistent
transliteration for less established Arabic names, without source-language
case endings or inserted explanations. A name’s etymological meaning should
not replace the name unless the passage explicitly uses wordplay.

The reviewer should check grammatical gender, plural implications,
case-governing prepositions, reflexivity, verb valency, and whether a compound
has an unintended everyday reading. Watch for overstrong doctrinal vocabulary,
false equivalence between `Glaube`, `Überzeugung`, `Treue`, and related terms,
and misleading legal register. Confirm that the result is concise, idiomatic
Standard German, preserves semantic roles and scope, and shows no Turkish
syntax beneath the surface.
