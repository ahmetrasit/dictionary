# Western Punjabi, Shahmukhi script (`pnb-Arab`)

Write Western Punjabi in the Shahmukhi form of the Arabic-derived script.
Use broadly intelligible formal Pakistani Punjabi rather than a narrowly local
dialect. The written standard is less unified than those of many national
languages, so apply one consistent mainstream Shahmukhi orthography and do
not silently replace Punjabi with Urdu.

Use compact Punjabi noun or adjective phrases and natural verbal labels.
Preserve grammatical gender, number, oblique forms, postposition government,
transitivity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state, process,
result, agent, and patient. Perfective transitive constructions may show
ergative alignment, and Punjabi compound or vector verbs can add completion,
direction, intensity, or affectedness. Choose these by Punjabi usage rather
than surface similarity to Urdu or Hindi. Contextual glosses may identify a
participant or postposition where a bare item is too broad. Standard
Shahmukhi does not fully represent all tonal contrasts, so do not invent
spellings to force them.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, postpositional frame, vector-verb
behavior, and construction-bound limits rather than generalize a local use.

Arabic- and Persian-derived words are legitimate in Shahmukhi Punjabi, but
graphic similarity to Arabic evidence does not make a source word a Punjabi
gloss. Use only established Punjabi lexemes with the required Punjabi sense
and morphology. Never paste Arabic phrases or preserve Arabic case endings.
Urdu religious vocabulary may be understandable yet non-Punjabi or
semantically institutionalized; check actual Punjabi naturalness. Never
mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds, light verbs, or suffix
patterns, including via Urdu.

Use conventional Punjabi Shahmukhi forms for established scriptural and
historical proper names. Spell less familiar names consistently without
source inflection or unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a proper name with
its etymological meaning unless explicit wordplay requires it.

Review Shahmukhi spelling, Punjabi-versus-Urdu lexical choice, gender, oblique
forms, postpositions, alignment, causativity, and vector-verb meaning. Check
Arabic/Persian loans for Punjabi semantic drift while recognizing that
legitimate target wording necessarily shares the Arabic-derived script.
Reject Urdu prose, copied Arabic evidence, and Turkish calques. Confirm a
compact, idiomatic Western Punjabi gloss, and note material choices created by
the locale’s limited orthographic standardization.
