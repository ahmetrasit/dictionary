# Uzbek, Latin script (`uz-Latn`)

Write contemporary Standard Uzbek as used in Uzbekistan, using the official
Latin orthography. Use `oʻ` and `gʻ` consistently with the modifier apostrophe
and preserve `sh`, `ch`, and other standard digraphs. Do not mix Cyrillic
letters, ad hoc ASCII spellings, or Turkish alphabet conventions.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and `-moq` infinitives for verbal
concepts when natural. Preserve case government, possessive marking, voice,
reflexivity, reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state,
process, result, agent, and patient. Uzbek does not apply vowel harmony as
productively as many related Turkic languages; do not import foreign harmony
patterns. Participles and verbal nouns can encode argument relations
compactly, but verify their head and temporal or modal implications.
Contextual glosses may specify an object or complement when a short root is
too broad. Choose auxiliary and compound verbs by established Uzbek usage.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, suffixation, argument frame, and
construction-bound limits rather than generalize a contextual reading.

Do not use Turkish as a word-substitution template. Uzbek and Turkish
cognates can diverge in meaning, register, valency, and collocation, while
apparently transparent compounds may be Turkish calques. Check every close
cognate independently and prefer native Uzbek phrasing. Arabic and Persian
loans may have entered through distinct historical paths, and Russian loans
may add Soviet or administrative senses; test each in modern Uzbek. Arabic
evidence must not be copied or merely transliterated.

Use conventional Uzbek forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. Render other names consistently in Uzbek Latin spelling,
without Arabic case endings, Russian intermediary spelling, or unrequested
honorifics. Do not replace a proper name with its etymology unless contextual
wordplay is explicit.

Review orthography and apostrophes, case, possessives, voice, causativity,
compound-verb collocation, and category. Search specifically for Turkish
false friends, Turkish suffix calques, and accidental Kazakh or Russian
forms. Check Arabic/Persian religious vocabulary for Uzbek semantic drift.
Confirm that the result is concise, idiomatic Standard Uzbek and not Turkish
recoded into Uzbek-looking morphology.
