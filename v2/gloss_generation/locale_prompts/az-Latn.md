# Azerbaijani, Latin script (`az-Latn`)

Write contemporary Standard Azerbaijani as used in the Republic of
Azerbaijan, using the official Latin alphabet. Preserve Azerbaijani letters
and spelling, including `ə`, `ğ`, `ı`, `ö`, `ş`, `ü`, `ç`, `x`, and `q`.
Do not substitute Turkey Turkish forms or silently use Cyrillic.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and `-maq/-mək` infinitives for verbal
concepts when natural. Preserve vowel harmony, case government, possessive
marking, voice, reflexivity, reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among
action, state, process, result, agent, and patient. Azerbaijani participles,
verbal nouns, and possessive compounds can compress relations, but the short
form must keep the correct head and participant roles. Contextual glosses may
name a governed object or complement when a bare cognate is polysemous.
Choose auxiliary and light-verb constructions by Azerbaijani collocation, not
by formal resemblance to another language.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, suffixation, valency, and
construction-bound scope rather than promote a local use to the concept.

Turkish is an especially dangerous pivot here: close cognates can differ in
frequency, register, valency, connotation, or basic sense, and a grammatical
word-for-word rendering can still be non-Azerbaijani. Never mechanically copy
Turkish word order, compounds, suffix choices, or idioms. Check every
Turkish-looking item as an Azerbaijani lexeme. Arabic and Persian loans also
require target-language verification; inherited cognates may have drifted or
developed specifically Azerbaijani religious and administrative meanings.
Do not use raw Arabic evidence or Ottoman/Turkish spelling as a gloss.

Use conventional Azerbaijani forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. For less familiar names, apply consistent Azerbaijani Latin
spelling without Arabic case endings or unrequested honorifics. Do not
translate a name’s etymology unless the context explicitly activates it.

Review alphabet choice, vowel harmony, case, possessive construction, voice,
valency, and compound headedness. Compare likely Turkish cognates against
actual Azerbaijani meaning and collocation; flag false friends and
Turkish-calqued phrases even when immediately intelligible. Check Russian,
Arabic, and Persian loans for register or semantic drift. Confirm a concise,
idiomatic Azerbaijani gloss that preserves the branch boundary independently
of Turkish.
