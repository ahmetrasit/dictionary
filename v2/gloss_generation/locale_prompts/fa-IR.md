# Iranian Persian (`fa-IR`)

Write contemporary Iranian Persian in the Persian form of the Arabic-derived
script, with current Iranian orthographic conventions. Prefer educated,
widely understood Iranian usage; avoid unnecessary classical ornament,
Afghan-specific Dari vocabulary, and colloquial Tehran forms unless the
concept demands them.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural Persian verbal labels.
Apply ezafe clearly and avoid long chains whose semantic head becomes
uncertain. Persian light-verb constructions with کردن، شدن، دادن، زدن, and
others are lexical choices: preserve transitivity, causativity, inchoative
meaning, agency, and event structure rather than swapping light verbs freely.
Distinguish action from result, quality from bearer, and active from passive
orientation. Contextual glosses may state a complement or participant when a
bare noun or adjective would broaden the concept. Use نیم‌فاصله and plural or
derivational morphology consistently where standard.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, complement frame, light-verb
collocation, and construction-bound scope rather than generalize a contextual
use.

Arabic-derived vocabulary is fully embedded in Persian, but cognate shape
does not guarantee the Arabic source sense. Distinguish legitimate Persian
words in Arabic-derived script from copied Arabic evidence. Do not paste
Arabic clauses, retain Arabic case endings, or choose an obscure learned
cognate solely for graphic similarity. Religious, legal, and philosophical
loans may have Persian institutional meanings that narrow or broaden the
branch. Prefer idiomatic Persian, and never mechanically copy Turkish word
order, possessive patterns, light verbs, or explanatory phrasing.

Use conventional Iranian Persian forms for familiar proper names. For less
established names, preserve a consistent Persian spelling without source
inflection or added honorifics. Do not translate a name’s etymology unless the
context explicitly activates lexical wordplay.

Review Persian spelling (`ی` and `ک`, spacing, نیم‌فاصله), ezafe, plural
formation, light-verb selection, valency, and register. Test Arabic cognates
against actual Iranian Persian meanings and distinguish acceptable Persian
script from verbatim Arabic source leakage. Check for accidental Dari,
classical, or Turkish-shaped wording. The final gloss should be compact,
idiomatic Iranian Persian and preserve the semantic center and roles.
