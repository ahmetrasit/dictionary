# Spanish (`es`)

Use contemporary neutral Standard Spanish in Latin script. The unregioned
`es` locale should be broadly intelligible in Spain and the Americas, so avoid
country-specific colloquialisms, regional second-person forms, and senses that
are marked in only one major variety. Choose a compact dictionary register
rather than archaic biblical prose.

Prefer short noun phrases, adjective phrases, or infinitives for concept
glosses. Articles are normally unnecessary in labels unless they resolve
reference or countability. Retain reflexive `se` when it marks a lexicalized,
inchoative, reciprocal, or anticausative sense. Preserve transitivity,
participant roles, and distinctions among act, process, state, result, and
agent. Do not casually interchange `ser` and `estar` readings or replace a
causative with `hacer` plus an imprecise adjective. Contextual glosses may
state a governed preposition or object when the bare lemma is too broad.
Avoid excessive nominalization and source-like strings of `de`. Lexical-unit
glosses must keep the unit’s category, argument frame, and construction-bound
limits instead of converting a local sense into a general concept.

Prefer established Spanish vocabulary. Use Arabic-derived terms only when
they are genuinely current Spanish words with the required sense; historical
Arabic origin alone is irrelevant, and Islamic technical loans often vary by
community. Transliteration should not hide the absence of a real translation.
Guard against theological or legal terms whose Spanish usage has acquired
Christian, canon-law, or modern political associations. Arabic source wording
is evidence, not target text. Never copy Turkish syntax, possessive compounds,
light verbs, or productive suffixes mechanically into Spanish.

Use conventional Spanish forms of familiar proper names, including
scriptural figures where a stable form exists. Less familiar Arabic names
should follow one readable Spanish-oriented transliteration system. Do not
translate a name’s etymology, append honorifics, or alternate spellings within
one locale.

Review gender and number agreement, reflexivity, prepositions, aspectual
implications, and whether a label is neutral across regions. Look for false
friends, especially international religious vocabulary, and for broad words
such as `fe`, `caridad`, `piedad`, or `prueba` that can suppress relevant
facets. Confirm idiomatic Spanish, correct semantic scope and roles, and the
absence of Turkish-shaped phrasing.
