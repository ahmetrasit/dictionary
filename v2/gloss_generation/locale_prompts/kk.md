# Kazakh (`kk`)

Write contemporary Standard Kazakh in the currently established Kazakh
Cyrillic orthography. The bare `kk` locale does not explicitly select a
script; this pack treats Cyrillic as the deployment default. A Latin-script
release should instead be identified and reviewed as `kk-Latn`. Preserve
Kazakh-specific letters such as `ә`, `ғ`, `қ`, `ң`, `ө`, `ұ`, `ү`, `һ`, and
`і`.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural infinitival verbal labels.
Preserve vowel and consonant harmony, case government, possessive marking,
voice, reflexivity, reciprocity, causativity, and distinctions among action,
state, process, result, agent, and patient. Kazakh participles and verbal
nouns may imply tense, modality, or argument structure; do not use them as
interchangeable abbreviations. Contextual glosses may state an object or
relation when a short root is too polysemous. Check actual Kazakh auxiliary
and compound-verb collocations.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, suffixation, valency, and
construction-bound scope rather than elevate a local use.

Related Turkic forms are a major risk. Never mechanically copy Turkish word
order, compounds, suffixes, or idioms; a recognizable cognate may be a false
friend or differ in valency and register. Also avoid substituting Kyrgyz,
Uzbek, or Tatar-looking forms. Arabic and Persian religious loans may have
Kazakh-specific senses, while Russian-mediated terminology can carry Soviet,
legal, or institutional implications. Evaluate the modern Kazakh lexeme, not
its source resemblance, and do not copy Arabic evidence.

Use conventional Kazakh forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. For unfamiliar names, follow consistent Kazakh Cyrillic
adaptation rather than Russian transcription, omitting Arabic case endings
and unrequested honorifics. Do not translate a name’s etymology unless
wordplay is explicitly active.

Review script, Kazakh-specific letters, harmony, case, possessives, voice,
valency, and compound headedness. Check every Turkish-like cognate for false
friendship and every Russian or Arabic loan for semantic drift. Reject
Russian-calqued or Turkish-calqued phrasing. Confirm compact, idiomatic Kazakh
and record the Cyrillic-default assumption when assessing deployment.
