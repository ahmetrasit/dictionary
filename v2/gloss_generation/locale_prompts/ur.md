# Urdu (`ur`)

Write in contemporary Standard Urdu in the Perso-Arabic Urdu script, following
normal Urdu spelling (typically displayed in Nastaliq). Use an educated,
widely intelligible register associated with Pakistan and transregional Urdu;
avoid both opaque Persianization and unnecessary substitution with highly
Sanskritized Hindi.

Form concept glosses as compact Urdu noun or adjective phrases and natural
verbal expressions. Preserve grammatical gender, number, postposition
government, transitivity, causativity, and distinctions among state, process,
result, agent, and patient. Urdu compound verbs and light verbs such as کرنا،
ہونا، دینا، and لینا can alter aspect, agency, completion, or affectedness;
choose them for their Urdu meaning, not as automatic counterparts of a source
construction. Use izafat where standard and readable, but avoid long
Persian-style chains that conceal the head. Contextual glosses may name a
participant or postpositional relation when a short lemma is overly broad.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve category, postpositional frame,
compound-verb behavior, and construction-bound limits rather than generalize
a local reading.

Arabic and Persian vocabulary is a legitimate part of Urdu, including in
Arabic-derived script. Distinguish natural Urdu wording from copied Arabic
evidence: a source word becomes acceptable only if it is an established Urdu
lexeme with the required Urdu sense and morphology. Do not paste Arabic
phrases, retain source inflection, or prefer an obscure cognate simply because
it resembles the evidence. Familiar religious terms may have ritual, legal,
sectarian, or South Asian meanings that drift from the source branch. Never
mechanically copy Turkish syntax, compounds, or light-verb choices.

Use conventional Urdu forms for established scriptural and historical proper
names. Preserve standard Urdu spellings rather than retransliterating them
letter by letter; omit Arabic case endings and unrequested honorifics. Do not
replace a proper name with its literal meaning unless contextual wordplay
requires it.

Review spelling, letter distinctions, gender agreement, plural behavior,
izafat, postpositions, causative level, and compound-verb aspect. Check every
Arabic-looking term for actual Urdu naturalness and semantic range, while
never treating legitimate Urdu script as leakage merely because it resembles
Arabic. Reject Hindi calques, copied Arabic evidence, and Turkish-shaped
phrasing; confirm a concise, idiomatic Urdu dictionary gloss.
