# Tajik, Cyrillic script (`tg-Cyrl`)

Write contemporary Standard Tajik in the official Tajik Cyrillic alphabet,
including `ғ`, `ӣ`, `қ`, `ӯ`, `ҳ`, and `ҷ`. Do not output Persian script or
substitute Russian spellings. Use the modern Tajik literary standard and avoid
strongly regional colloquialisms.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and natural Tajik verbal labels.
Preserve the ezafe linker `-и`, case-like prepositional relations, light-verb
collocation, transitivity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state,
process, result, agent, and patient. Tajik compound verbs with elements such
as `кардан` and `шудан` are lexical and event-structural choices, not freely
interchangeable. Contextual glosses may make an argument or relation explicit
where a short Persian cognate has too wide a sense. Avoid long learned ezafe
chains and Russian-shaped nominal syntax.

Lexical-unit glosses must retain category, complement frame, light-verb
collocation, and construction-bound limits rather than generalize a local
use.

Arabic-derived words are deeply integrated in Tajik, but they must be judged
by modern Tajik spelling and meaning. Do not copy Arabic evidence, reproduce
Arabic script, or choose a rare cognate solely because its etymon matches.
Iranian Persian and Dari cognates can differ in register or collocation, while
Russian loans may introduce Soviet or administrative senses. Turkish is not
a safe syntactic pivot: never mechanically transfer Turkish word order,
compounds, suffixal abstractions, or light-verb choices into Tajik.

Use conventional Tajik forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. For less familiar names, use consistent Tajik Cyrillic adaptation,
without Arabic case endings, Persian-script fragments, or unrequested
honorifics. Do not replace a name with its lexical etymology unless explicit
wordplay requires it.

Review Tajik-specific letters, ezafe, spacing, light verbs, valency, and
register. Check for accidental Russian, Iranian Persian, or Uzbek forms and
for Arabic loans whose Tajik meaning has drifted. Reject Turkish-shaped
phrasing even when semantically guessable. Confirm a concise, idiomatic
Standard Tajik gloss with the proper semantic head and participants.
