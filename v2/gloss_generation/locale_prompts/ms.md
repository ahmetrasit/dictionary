# Malay (`ms`)

Write contemporary Standard Malay in Latin `Rumi` script. Because `ms` has no
region subtag, prefer forms broadly acceptable in formal Malay, with
Malaysia-oriented standard spelling where a choice is unavoidable; avoid
colloquial regional forms and do not silently produce Indonesian. A future
deployment needing a stricter national norm should use a region-specific tag.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and idiomatic verbal labels. Select
affixes deliberately: `meN-`, `ber-`, `ter-`, `di-`, `memper-`, `-kan`,
`-i`, `peN-`, and `ke-…-an` affect voice, valency, agency, result, and state.
An unaffixed root is not always an acceptable standalone gloss. Preserve the
difference between accidental and stative `ter-`, process and outcome, and
agent and instrument. Use reduplication only when lexicalized or semantically
required. Contextual glosses may state a complement when the short form would
be too broad.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve affixation, category, valency, and
construction-bound scope rather than elevate a local reading.

Arabic loans are deeply established in Malay Islamic vocabulary, but local
ritual, legal, or institutional meanings can differ from the source Arabic.
Use standard Malay spelling and actual Malay semantics; raw transliteration
or Arabic script is not appropriate for this locale. English and Indonesian
near-equivalents also need checking for shifted register. Do not over-Arabize
the wording, and never mechanically copy Turkish word order, compounds,
light-verb expressions, or productive nominalizations.

Use conventional Malay forms for established scriptural and historical
proper names. Less familiar Arabic names should follow consistent Rumi
spelling, without source case endings, gratuitous apostrophes, or added
honorifics. Do not replace a name with its etymology unless explicit wordplay
requires it.

Review affix choice, nasal assimilation, voice, valency, reduplication,
standard spelling, and Malay—not Indonesian—collocation. Check Arabic-derived
terms for semantic drift and whether a familiar religious label is narrower
than the branch. Confirm a concise, idiomatic Standard Malay gloss, while
recording that the unregioned locale leaves some Malaysia/Brunei/Singapore
standard preferences inherently open.
