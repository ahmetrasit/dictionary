# Bosnian (`bs`)

Write contemporary Standard Bosnian in the Latin alphabet, using the
Ijekavian standard and Bosnian orthographic conventions. Preserve letters and
digraphs such as `č`, `ć`, `dž`, `đ`, `lj`, `nj`, `š`, and `ž`. Closely
related Croatian, Serbian, and Montenegrin forms may be intelligible, but
select wording natural and standard in Bosnian.

Use compact noun or adjective phrases and infinitives for verbal concepts.
Preserve verbal aspect, reflexive `se`, case government, animacy, gender,
transitivity, causativity, and distinctions among action, state, process,
result, agent, and patient. A perfective verb can add completion or
boundedness, while a reflexive form may be lexical, reciprocal, middle, or
passive-like; choose deliberately. Contextual glosses may state a governed
participant or preposition when the bare lemma has several frames. Avoid
source-shaped genitive chains and heavy abstract nominalization.

Lexical-unit glosses must preserve aspect, category, case frame, and
construction-bound scope rather than elevate a contextual use.

Bosnian contains many Arabic- and Persian-derived words transmitted through
Ottoman Turkish. These are genuine Bosnian lexemes only in their established
Bosnian form and meaning; cognate history does not prove present semantic fit.
Some carry specifically Islamic, historical, colloquial, or regional
associations. Do not revive an Ottomanism or copy Arabic evidence merely
because it resembles the source. Never mechanically map Turkish word order,
compounds, light verbs, or suffixes into Bosnian. Christian and Yugoslav
administrative vocabulary can also add unintended institutional meanings.

Use conventional Bosnian forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. Apply one consistent Bosnian spelling to less familiar names without
Arabic case endings or unrequested honorifics. Do not replace a name with its
etymology unless the context explicitly activates wordplay.

Review Ijekavian forms, spelling, aspect, case, animacy, reflexivity, valency,
and semantic role. Check Ottoman-derived vocabulary for Bosnian semantic
drift and Turkish-calque risk; check that a seemingly common BCS word has the
intended Bosnian register. Reject accidental Ekavian and copied Croatian or
Serbian standard preferences where Bosnian differs. Confirm concise,
idiomatic Standard Bosnian with no Turkish syntactic skeleton.
