# Polish (`pl`)

Use contemporary Standard Polish in Latin script and a neutral lexicographic
register. Avoid archaic biblical vocabulary, highly literary synonyms, and
Catholic institutional terminology unless those meanings are genuinely part
of the represented concept.

Concept labels should be concise nouns or noun phrases, adjective phrases, or
infinitives. Preserve aspect: a perfective and an imperfective verb do not
normally describe the same event profile. Retain reflexive `się` when it marks
a lexicalized, reciprocal, anticausative, or middle sense. Respect case
government, animacy, grammatical gender, and distinctions among action,
process, state, result, agent, and patient. Do not use an agentive masculine
personal noun as a supposedly neutral action label. Contextual glosses may
state the governed participant when a bare Polish lemma would be too broad.
Avoid unnatural chains of genitives and overproductive abstract nouns.
Lexical-unit glosses must retain aspect, category, argument frame, and
construction-bound scope rather than elevate a contextual reading.

Prefer established Polish wording to transliterated Arabic. Arabic-Islamic
terms used in scholarship or journalism may be unfamiliar in general Polish
or may carry modern political associations. Treat them as loans only when
their Polish meaning precisely fits. Christian near-equivalents deserve equal
caution because words shaped by Catholic usage can shift the semantic center.
Arabic material is evidence, not target-language text. Never mechanically
transfer Turkish word order, possessive compounds, light-verb constructions,
or suffixal nominalizations.

Use conventional Polish forms for familiar scriptural and historical proper
names. For less established Arabic names, use one consistent Polish-readable
transliteration; avoid source case endings, gratuitous variants, and
honorifics. Do not decline a newly transliterated name inconsistently, and do
not replace a name with its etymology.

Review aspect, case, animacy, gender, reflexivity, and verb valency. Check that
a chosen noun does not imply a person, institution, or completed result absent
from the branch. Scrutinize high-risk religious words such as `wiara`,
`miłosierdzie`, `pobożność`, and `pokusa` for inherited doctrinal scope.
Reject surface-fluent calques and confirm that the result is compact,
idiomatic Polish with the correct semantic roles and no Turkish syntactic
residue.
