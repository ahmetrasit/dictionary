# Swedish (`sv`)

Write in contemporary Standard Swedish using Latin script. The `sv` tag is
not region-specific, so prefer broadly standard wording rather than marked
Sweden-only slang or Finland-Swedish administrative vocabulary. Use modern
lexicographic prose, not archaic Bible style.

Form concept glosses as concise nouns or noun phrases, adjective phrases, or
infinitives. Swedish compounds are effective when established and
unambiguous; avoid invented compounds whose first element can be read in the
wrong relation to the head. Preserve particle verbs, reflexive forms, verb
valency, definiteness, and distinctions among action, state, result, agent,
and patient. Articles are often omitted in dictionary labels, but do not
erase a required relational preposition. Contextual glosses may make an object
or participant explicit when a bare word has too wide a range. Avoid turning
every concept into a heavy noun in `-het` or `-ande`. Lexical-unit glosses
must keep particle structure, category, valency, and any construction-bound
limit rather than generalize a contextual reading.

Prefer established Swedish equivalents. Arabic and Islamic loans used in
media or community contexts may have a specialized or politically loaded
Swedish sense, and a romanized source term is not automatically informative.
Inherited Christian vocabulary may add Lutheran or church-specific
associations. Use either only when the semantic boundary fits. Do not copy
Arabic phrases or mimic their structure. Never mechanically translate Turkish
compound order, possessive relations, light verbs, or productive
nominalizations.

Use conventional Swedish forms for familiar scriptural and historical names.
For other Arabic names, use one consistent Swedish-readable transliteration,
without source inflection or added honorifics. Do not substitute a name’s
literal etymology unless contextual wordplay requires it.

Review compound headedness, particle placement, reflexivity, definiteness,
gender, and whether verb choice changes telicity or participant roles.
Scrutinize words such as `tro`, `barmhärtighet`, `fromhet`, and `frestelse`,
whose Christian or everyday profiles may not align with the source. Confirm
that each phrase is idiomatic, compact Standard Swedish, retains the relevant
facets, and does not reveal Turkish syntax under Swedish vocabulary.
